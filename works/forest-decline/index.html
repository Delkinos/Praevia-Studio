Where the trees are going

Deforestation is rarely one villain. It's a handful of pressures, stacking up on the same land.

field

Agricultural expansion

Cropland and cattle pasture drive the largest share of tropical forest loss, especially across the Amazon and Southeast Asia.

timber

Logging & timber

Legal and illegal logging removes old growth for lumber, paper pulp, and fuelwood, often opening roads that invite further clearing.

ore

Mining & extraction

Mineral and oil extraction clears forest directly and fragments what's left, cutting corridors that wildlife can no longer cross.

fire

Fire

Land is often cleared with fire, and a warming, drier climate makes those fires more likely to spread beyond where they were set.

grid

Urban & infrastructure growth

Roads, dams, and expanding settlements push into forest edges, converting land in ways that are rarely reversed.

crop

Cash crops

Palm oil, soy, coffee, and cocoa grown for export are among the most common replacements for cleared tropical forest.

What's lost with the canopy

Carbon released, not stored

Standing forests hold carbon for centuries. Cleared and burned, that carbon returns to the atmosphere — forests currently offset only a fraction of what they once absorbed.

Species with nowhere to go

Forests hold most of the planet's land-based species. Fragmented habitat isolates populations until they can no longer sustain themselves.

Disrupted rainfall

Large forests generate their own rain by recycling moisture inland. Remove enough of the canopy and the rainfall pattern it created weakens too.

Soil that washes away

Roots hold slopes together. Without them, topsoil erodes within a few seasons, silting rivers and lowering the land's ability to regrow.

Communities displaced

Indigenous and forest-dependent communities lose the land their food, water, and livelihoods depend on — often with the least say in the decision.

What actually slows this down

Not every well-meaning action moves the needle equally. These do.

Look for FSC or Rainforest Alliance labels

On wood, paper, and palm-oil products, these certifications mean the supply chain was audited against deforestation-free sourcing rules.

Cut back on the highest-footprint foods

Beef and soy (mostly grown for animal feed) are linked to more tropical deforestation than almost any other food category.

Back land rights for indigenous communities

Forests under indigenous stewardship consistently show lower deforestation rates than surrounding land — securing their land titles protects the forest too.

Support verified restoration, not just planting pledges

A tree planted isn't a forest restored. Favor organizations that track long-term survival rates, not just sapling counts.

Push for policy, not just personal habits

Supply-chain deforestation laws and protected-area enforcement shift far more land than individual consumption choices ever can alone.
